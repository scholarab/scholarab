# Matching usability sessions

Status: pending at the user's request on September 10, 2026. No participants recruited or contacted. Automated browser/axe checks and internal walkthroughs are not participant results.

## Two rounds

Recruit 5–8 consenting participants per round when separately authorized. Include Grade 10, 11 and 12, post-secondary/other pathways, rural communities and uncertain plans. Invite participants who use keyboard or assistive technology when feasible. Do not collect real identity, financial, health or family circumstances. Use the synthetic scenarios below. Arrange appropriate consent for participants who are minors before sessions begin.

Round 1 evaluates comprehension and navigation. Fix observed issues before Round 2. Round 2 verifies the fixes with different participants where possible. A facilitator observes without explaining labels or directing clicks. Record aggregate task outcomes and anonymous notes; no names, emails, browser histories or actual student profiles in repository files.

## Session script (20–25 minutes)

1. “You are a Grade 12 student in Calgary considering scholarships and programs. Start a search. Talk through what you expect your answers to change.”
2. “Find a listing worth investigating. Explain what ‘Worth checking’ tells you, and whether the result confirms you can apply.”
3. “Check its requirement explanation and application timing. Identify what the provider still needs to confirm.”
4. “Compare three opportunities and choose a next step. Explain why your choice fits this scenario.”
5. “Save one listing. Find the saved application tools and calendar option. Find where you could request a reminder, without entering an email or submitting anything.”
6. “Change your plans or community. Find and edit your answers. Describe which earlier answers you expect to be cleared.”
7. On a clearly labeled synthetic reviewed fixture: “Try an optional refinement. Skip the personal topic. Explain whether you believe the system inferred your answer.”
8. “You need more time. Find the session extension control. Then end the answer session. Explain what should remain saved.”

Do not imply that current unreviewed data can produce verified recommendations. Use an isolated fixture for refinement when live data has no supported questions; make its synthetic nature explicit.

## Review decision

Any participant interpreting an unresolved badge as guaranteed eligibility is a blocking comprehension issue. Any inability to decline, edit, reach all results or clear answers is a blocking interaction issue. Report actual participant counts and task denominators. Do not turn a small convenience sample into a population success-rate claim. Human usability and real screen-reader validation stay pending until sessions occur.
